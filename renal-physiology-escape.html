Renal Physiology meets clinical MCQs

So now you are all across the nephron basics — let's make this clinically relevant

Which of the following explains the elevated creatinine associated with trimethoprim?
AAcute interstitial nephritis secondary to trimethoprim
BDirect proximal tubular cell toxicity causing structural nephron loss
CInhibition of tubular secretion of creatinine by competitive blockade of proximal tubule transporter proteins
DAfferent arteriolar constriction causing reduced glomerular filtration
EIntratubular crystal precipitation causing obstructive nephropathy
Trimethoprim competitively inhibits OCT2 (organic cation transporter 2) and MATE1/2 transporters expressed on the basolateral and luminal membranes of the proximal convoluted tubule. These are the same transporters responsible for secreting creatinine into the tubular lumen — so blocking them raises serum creatinine without any true reduction in GFR.

The hyperkalaemia is a separate, well-recognised effect — trimethoprim also blocks ENaC channels in the collecting duct, mimicking amiloride.

⚠️ Note: Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim) can also cause acute interstitial nephritis — so AIN remains on the differential in the right clinical context.

Frusemide Facts

Which of the following statements regarding frusemide are correct? Select all that apply (3 TRUE, 2 FALSE)
✓
Frusemide is freely filtered at the glomerulus due to its low molecular weight
✓

✓
Frusemide reaches the tubular lumen via active secretion through organic anion transporters (OAT1/OAT3) in the proximal tubule
✓
Frusemide inhibits the NKCC2 co-transporter on the luminal surface of the thick ascending limb of the Loop of Henle
✓
Frusemide is predominantly hepatically metabolised
Furosemide is a loop diuretic which inhibits the NKCC2 transporter in the thick ascending limb of the Loop of Henle. Around 90% of frusemide in the blood is bound to albumin — so it is not freely filtered at the glomerulus. How does it arrive in the loop of Henle? Frusemide is secreted into the proximal tubule via organic anion transporters (OAT1/OAT3) on the basolateral membrane, and exits via MRP2/4 on the apical membrane into the tubular lumen. The majority of frusemide is then excreted unchanged in the urine — not hepatically metabolised.
